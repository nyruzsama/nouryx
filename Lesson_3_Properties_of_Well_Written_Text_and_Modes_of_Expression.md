## 🏛️ PART 1: PROPERTIES OF A WELL-WRITTEN TEXT

### Foundations of Clear Writing
- **Writing** is a form of communication intended to **inform, inspire, or entertain** readers.
- However, to achieve these intended goals of writing, a text needs to have **UNITY and CONNECTEDNESS**.

#### Core Concepts:
- **Unity**: Refers to how well the parts of a text fit together to form a whole. In writing, you achieve unity when:
  - All sentences develop the main idea.
  - Each paragraph deals with a single main idea.
- **Connectedness**: Refers to being able to weave your ideas in such a way that they flow logically, ensuring the whole text makes sense.

To ensure that your ideas are unified and connected, you have to make sure that your text has the three properties of a well-written text:
1. **Organization**
2. **Coherence and Cohesion**
3. **Appropriate Language**

---

### 1. ORGANIZATION

#### Definition & Key Distinction:
- **ORGANIZATION** is the logical arrangement of ideas in a text to make it easier for readers to understand the message.
- On the other hand, **ORDER** refers to your choice of what to mention first, second, next, and so on.

#### 3-Part Structural Division:
A very common way to organize the ideas of your text is to divide them into three parts:
1. **Introduction**:
   - Presents the main idea or central point.
   - Often includes a hook to grab the reader's attention.
2. **Body**:
   - Contains the most details about the experience.
   - Expounds on the main idea through a series of events or supporting details arranged logically.
3. **Conclusion**:
   - Gives closure to the text by summarizing major points or posing a reflective question for the reader to ponder.

---

#### 📐 Organizational Patterns & Exact Slide Examples:

Another common way to organize the ideas of your text is to use organizational patterns:
- **Inductive**
- **Deductive**
- **Chronological Order**
- **Spatial Order**
- **Order of Importance**

---

#### A. INDUCTIVE ORGANIZATION
- **Definition**: Proceeds from specific details to general ideas; you write the specific details first and then summarize everything as a main idea at the end.
- **Visual Pattern**: Triangle Pointing UP (Apex = **SPECIFIC** at top $\rightarrow$ Base = **BROAD** at bottom).
- **Exact Verbatim Example**:
  > *"I began my day at the Mercado, weaving between stalls where vendors offered ripe lanzones, fresh tilapia, and boxed buko pie while customers chatted about neighborhood news. Midday I visited the Bahay ni Rizal, taking in the reconstructed ancestral home and its exhibits that trace José Rizal's childhood and family life. In the afternoon I lingered at the town plaza, where dozens of vendors sold kwek-kwek, grilled squid, taho, and silog plates under colorful tarps as families and students passed by. Those close-up scenes—market camaraderie, the calm historical museum, and the lively plaza food culture—convinced me that Calamba is a city grounded in community, history, and everyday warmth."*

---

#### B. DEDUCTIVE ORGANIZATION
- **Definition**: Proceeds from general to specific ideas; you express the main idea at the start and follow it with details, examples, and illustrations.
- **Visual Pattern**: Inverted Triangle (Base = **BROAD** at top $\rightarrow$ Apex = **SPECIFIC** at bottom).
- **Exact Verbatim Example**:
  > *"Calamba presents itself as a city where strong community life and proud historical memory meet in everyday places. That shows in the Mercado, where neighbors barter for fresh fish, fruits, and the region's well-known buko pie while sharing news; at the Bahay ni Rizal, where the preserved family home and displays honor José Rizal's local roots; and around the town plaza, where vendors sell kwek-kwek, grilled seafood, taho, and silog to passersby gathered beneath trees. These concrete scenes—the bustling market, the respectful shrine, and the vibrant street-food plaza—make Calamba's blend of communal warmth and historical pride clear."*

---

#### C. CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER
- **Definition**: Used to explain the history of an event, tell a story, relate an experience, or list steps in a process.
- **Exact Verbatim Example**:
  > *"The Bahay ni Rizal in Calamba began as the ancestral home where José Rizal was born in 1861; after his family left the house it passed through different owners and uses during the Spanish and American periods, then local historians and the municipality spearheaded restoration efforts in the 20th century to reconstruct the nipa-and-bamboo structure based on archival descriptions, and today the site functions as a shrine and museum that preserves artifacts, displays Rizal family memorabilia, and hosts educational programs."*

---

#### D. SPATIAL ORDER
- **Definition**: Used to help readers visualize something as you want them to see it or to create a main impression using the senses.
- **Exact Verbatim Example**:
  > *"Entering the Bahay ni Rizal complex, you first encounter the museum pavilion with display cases of Rizal's family photographs and childhood keepsakes, then the reconstructed nipa-and-bamboo house stands on raised posts surrounded by a small garden showcasing period plants, and beyond that the plaza area and memorial markers lead visitors toward the adjacent park and interpretive panels that place the site in Calamba's historic and civic landscape."*

---

#### E. ORDER OF IMPORTANCE
- **Definition**: Used to persuade or convince an audience by ranking items by their significance, benefit, or priority (Priority #1, Priority #2, Priority #3).
- **Exact Verbatim Example**:
  > *"Most important about the Bahay ni Rizal is its role as a tangible link to José Rizal's early life—serving as a focal point for national memory and civic education; next is its function as a museum that preserves and interprets family artifacts and local history for students and visitors; finally, its surrounding grounds and community programs (commemorative events, guided tours, and school visits) provide accessible ways for residents and tourists to engage with Calamba's cultural heritage."*

### 2. COHERENCE AND COHESION

#### Definitions:
- **COHERENCE**: Refers to the overall oneness of ideas, sentences, and paragraphs where all relevant ideas stick together.
- **COHESION**: Refers to the grammatical and lexical linking within a text that holds clauses, sentences, and paragraphs together.

Common ways to achieve COHESION and COHERENCE include paying close attention to the following:
1. **Transitional Devices**
2. **Grammar and Mechanics**
3. **Language**

---

#### 1. Transitional Devices
- **Definition**: These are words and phrases that act as bridges between ideas to develop, relate, and move them.
- **Comprehensive Function Reference**:
  - *To add ideas*: and, also, besides, further, furthermore, in addition, moreover, next, too, first, second
  - *To contrast or conflict*: but, however, on the other hand, in contrast, nevertheless, on the contrary, yet, whereas, although
  - *To illustrate / give examples*: for example, for instance, specifically, to illustrate, in particular, namely, such as
  - *To show cause, effect, or result*: therefore, thus, consequently, as a result, hence, because, since, accordingly
  - *To summarize or conclude*: in conclusion, in summary, to sum up, finally, all in all, in short
  - *To indicate time*: meanwhile, subsequently, simultaneously, after, before, then, earlier, later
  - *To indicate space / location*: above, below, adjacent, beyond, nearby, opposite, across

---

#### 2. Grammar and Mechanics
- **Definition**: Adherence to formal grammatical and orthographic rules of written communication.
- **Key Areas**:
  - Adherence to **subject-verb agreement**.
  - Use of **proper punctuation marks** (periods, commas, semicolons, dashes, apostrophes).
  - Observance of **proper capitalization** (proper nouns, beginnings of sentences, acronyms).
  - Proper spelling, paragraph indentation, and spacing.

---

#### 3. Appropriate Language
- **Definition**: Language refers to the medium through which you transmit your message to your audience. In writing, your language must align with your intended purpose.
- **Key Considerations**:
  - **Levels of Formality**: Consider your audience's expectations (**Formal** or **Informal**). Avoid slang, abbreviations, and informal idioms in formal contexts; use approachable, authentic tones for personal narratives.
  - **Jargon**: Specialized language used by a specific group of individuals or professional fields. Avoid unnecessary jargon when writing for general audiences, or define it explicitly when used.
  - **Precision and Clarity**: Choose simple, specific, and concrete words to ensure the reader understands the message clearly and without ambiguity.

## 🎨 PART 2: MODES OF EXPRESSION

### Core Philosophy:
- Every person has a story worth telling, but the true impact of that story lies in the mode you choose to express it.
- **Expression** is the bridge between your inner world and your audience; it is the art of sharing your thoughts, emotions, and intentions clearly and purposefully.

You can express your story, ideas, or message in many ways including:
1. **Personal Narratives**
2. **Journal Entries**
3. **Multimodal Expression**

---

### 1. PERSONAL NARRATIVES
- **Definition**: A personal narrative is a text structure that tells a story or recounts a specific event that occurred at a particular place and time.
- **Subjective Narration**: This is an example of Subjective Narration. Unlike objective reports, this mode presents a story based mainly on your feelings and personal impressions.
- **Goal**: The goal is to write as if you were taking the reader to the scene so they can visualize and feel what it was like to be there.

#### Key Features:
1. **Perspective**: Told from your point of view using pronouns like `"I,"` `"me,"` and `"we"`.
2. **Recreation of Experience**: Allows you to recreate an event so readers can visualize and feel what it was like to be there.
3. **Sensory Details**: Using details of what you saw, heard, tasted, smelled, or felt to give life to the story.

---

### 2. JOURNAL ENTRIES
- **Definition**: A personal record of events, introspections, emotional responses, and forward-looking plans.

#### Essential Components:
1. **Today's Date**: Writing the date for each entry is crucial for tracking your personal progress and growth over time.
2. **Notable Experiences**: Documenting significant events—whether positive or negative—is a way to develop a more positive mindset and learn deeper lessons from challenges.
3. **Mood or Emotion**: Recording your emotional state at the time of writing helps you reflect on the connection between your specific experiences and how you felt about them.
4. **Thoughts and Reflections**: A journal entry should contain your internal processing of a topic or experience, serving as a form of self-analysis or self-assessment.
5. **Goals and Plans**: Journals serve as a venue to document personal goals, helping you stay motivated, accountable, and aware of the challenges you encounter while trying to achieve them.
6. **Drawings or Photos**: You may include sketches, drawings, or pictures to better express complex thoughts and feelings that words alone might not capture.

---

### 3. MULTIMODAL EXPRESSION
- **Definition**: Multimodal expression occurs when multiple channels or modes (text, audio, video, images) are used simultaneously to convey a unified message.
- **Digital Presence & Media**: Establishing a digital presence is a common way to execute this mode of expression. A very popular example includes **VLOGGING and media content**.
- **Ethical & Responsible Communication Principle**:
  > *"Remembering that every digital post leaves an imprint, you must **think before you click** to ensure your expression is ethical and responsible."*
