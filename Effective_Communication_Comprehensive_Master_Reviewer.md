## 3. LESSON 3 DEEP DIVE: PROPERTIES OF A WELL-WRITTEN TEXT

### Three Foundational Pillars:
1. **Organization**: Logical structural layout (Introduction, Body, Conclusion).
2. **Coherence & Cohesion**: Macro-level conceptual oneness and micro-level lexical linking.
3. **Appropriate Language**: Contextual register, jargon control, precision, and clarity.

### The 5 Organizational Patterns & Real-World Illustrations:

#### 1. Inductive Pattern (Specific $\rightarrow$ General)
- *Process*: Observation/Details $\rightarrow$ Analysis $\rightarrow$ Core Thesis Statement.
- *Slide Model*: Wandering through Calamba's Mercado, then Bahay ni Rizal, then town plaza $\rightarrow$ Concluding that Calamba is grounded in community, history, and warmth.

#### 2. Deductive Pattern (General $\rightarrow$ Specific)
- *Process*: Main Thesis Statement $\rightarrow$ Supporting Evidence 1 $\rightarrow$ Supporting Evidence 2 $\rightarrow$ Proof.
- *Slide Model*: Opening thesis: Calamba is a city where community life and historical memory meet $\rightarrow$ Followed by evidence in the Mercado, Bahay ni Rizal, and plaza food stalls.

#### 3. Chronological Pattern (Temporal Sequence)
- *Process*: Time $T_0 \rightarrow T_1 \rightarrow T_2 \rightarrow T_n$.
- *Slide Model*: Bahay ni Rizal's history from Rizal's birth in 1861, to Spanish/American period transfers, to 20th-century municipal reconstruction, to its modern museum status.

#### 4. Spatial Pattern (Sensory Geography)
- *Process*: Physical location progression (e.g., entrance $\rightarrow$ courtyard $\rightarrow$ interior $\rightarrow$ surrounding grounds).
- *Slide Model*: Entering the Bahay ni Rizal pavilion display cases, moving to the raised nipa-and-bamboo house and garden, proceeding out to the plaza memorial markers.

#### 5. Order of Importance Pattern (Hierarchy of Value)
- *Process*: Priority 1 (Most Critical) $\rightarrow$ Priority 2 (Intermediate) $\rightarrow$ Priority 3 (Supplementary).
- *Slide Model*: Most important: tangible link to Rizal's early life; next: museum preserving artifacts; finally: community grounds and tours for local tourists.

## 4. LESSON 3 DEEP DIVE: MODES OF EXPRESSION

### 1. Personal Narrative (Subjective Narration)
- Uses first-person pronouns (`"I," "me," "we"`).
- Recreates lived reality through vivid sensory details (visual, auditory, olfactory, tactile, gustatory).
- Bridges personal emotion with storytelling to allow the reader to "be in the room."

### 2. Journal Entry (6 Essential Components)
1. **Today's Date**: Essential baseline for longitudinal growth tracking.
2. **Notable Experiences**: Recording significant events (positive or negative) to cultivate positive mindset and extract life lessons.
3. **Mood or Emotion**: Documenting affective state to analyze triggers and emotional patterns.
4. **Thoughts & Reflections**: Internal self-analysis, processing, and sense-making.
5. **Goals & Plans**: Setting milestones to sustain accountability and drive motivation.
6. **Drawings or Photos**: Visual artifacts that capture nuanced feelings beyond text.

### 3. Multimodal Expression
- Synthesizes text, audio, video, graphics, and interactivity (e.g., YouTube vlogs, podcasts, infographics, social media essays).
- Golden Rule: **"Think before you click"** — recognizing the permanent digital footprint of every published post.

## 5. LESSON 4 DEEP DIVE: CORE PROCESSES & METACOGNITIVE FRAMEWORKS

### The 4 Core Processes of Communication:
1. **Interpreting**: Active cognitive decoding of verbal symbols, tone, facial expressions, and body language.
2. **Engaging**: Relational participation, establishing rapport, empathetic listening, and non-judgmental presence.
3. **Expressing**: Articulating internal states clearly, ethically, and persuasively across chosen channels.
4. **Reflecting**: Continuous self-audit of communication effectiveness and behavioral refinement.

### Metacognition & The Lucas (2020) Framework:
- Metacognitive communicators constantly ask themselves:
  - *Before*: What is my goal? Who is my audience? What tone is best?
  - *During*: Is my listener engaged? Are my non-verbal cues matching my words? Do I need to clarify?
  - *After*: What worked well? Where did confusion occur? How can I improve next time?

## 6. LESSON 4 DEEP DIVE: REFLECTIVE EXPRESSION & ACTIONABLE GROWTH

### The 3 Pillars of Reflective Expression:
- **Insight**: Deep discernment of motives, assumptions, and communication tendencies.
- **Vulnerability**: Authentic, courageous transparency that invites reciprocal trust without defensive posturing.
- **Connection**: Relational empathy that bridges diverse cultural and interpersonal backgrounds.

### The 4 Actionable Growth Strategies:
1. **Setting Clear Goals**: Formulating measurable, specific targets.
2. **Seeking Feedback**: Proactively soliciting honest critiques to uncover unconscious blind spots.
3. **Intentional Practice**: Deliberate, repetitive execution of refined skills in varied environments.
4. **Tracking Progress**: Longitudinal journaling and systematic behavioral audits.

## 7. COMPARATIVE MATRIX: ORGANIZATIONAL PATTERNS

| Pattern | Flow / Direction | Best Used For | Typical Signal Words |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inductive** | Specific $\rightarrow$ Broad | Narrative discoveries, scientific investigations, inductive reasoning | *those scenes convinced me, ultimately, in summary* |
| **Deductive** | Broad $\rightarrow$ Specific | Academic essays, persuasive presentations, executive summaries | *that shows in, for example, specifically, to illustrate* |
| **Chronological** | Time Sequence ($T_0 \rightarrow T_n$) | Historical accounts, process manuals, biographic timelines | *began in, after, then, in the 20th century, today* |
| **Spatial** | Physical / Sensory Layout | Descriptive tours, environment walkthroughs, scene setting | *entering, first encounter, stands on, beyond that* |
| **Order of Importance** | Hierarchy of Significance | Persuasive pitches, policy briefs, prioritization lists | *most important, next is, finally, primary, secondary* |

## 9. COMPREHENSIVE SELF-ASSESSMENT QUESTION BANK

1. **Q: What two foundational qualities must a text possess to fulfill its purpose of informing, inspiring, or entertaining?**  
   *A: Unity and Connectedness.*
2. **Q: What is the core difference between Organization and Order?**  
   *A: Organization is the logical arrangement of ideas to facilitate comprehension, while Order refers to the specific sequential choice of what to mention first, second, and next.*
3. **Q: Which organizational pattern begins with granular sensory details and closes with a broad thesis statement?**  
   *A: Inductive Organization.*
4. **Q: What are the three primary components of a standard organized text?**  
   *A: Introduction, Body, and Conclusion.*
5. **Q: What are the three key elements to monitor when striving for Appropriate Language?**  
   *A: Levels of Formality, Jargon, and Precision/Clarity.*
6. **Q: Why is Subjective Narration characteristic of Personal Narratives?**  
   *A: Because personal narratives recount specific lived events based primarily on personal impressions, emotions, and first-person perspectives.*
7. **Q: What are the 6 essential components of a well-maintained journal entry?**  
   *A: Today's Date, Notable Experiences, Mood/Emotion, Thoughts & Reflections, Goals & Plans, and Drawings/Photos.*
8. **Q: What ethical axiom is emphasized for Multimodal Expression in the digital age?**  
   *A: "Think before you click" — honoring the permanence of digital imprints.*
9. **Q: List the four core processes in the continuous communication loop.**  
   *A: Interpreting, Engaging, Expressing, and Reflecting.*
10. **Q: How does Lucas (2020) conceptualize reflective communication?**  
    *A: As a fundamental competency for ethical dialogue, interpersonal maturity, and continuous personal growth.*
